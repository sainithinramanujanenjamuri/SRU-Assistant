🔍 Context & Motivation 02 / 15

Problem Statement & Institutional Challenges

Why traditional document distribution and generic AI chatbots fail in university environments

📑
Information Silos
Academic policies, R23 regulations, syllabus codes, and exam calendars are scattered across 50+ page PDFs and circulating circulars, leading to high administrative friction.
⚠️
Parametric Hallucinations
Standard LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude) lack access to private university regulations. When asked about SR University rules, they hallucinate policies from other institutions.
🚫
Zero Provenance
Generic chatbots cannot cite official document names, sections, or paragraph numbers, leaving students and faculty unable to verify claims.
Core Capstone Objective: Develop an end-to-end, zero-cost, local Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) assistant that answers academic questions strictly grounded in official SR University documents with verifiable paragraph-level citations.
Presenter (Enjamuri Sai Nithin): "Every semester, hundreds of students visit academic offices asking: 'What is the condonation limit under R23?', 'What are the passing marks for CIE vs SEE?', or 'When are Mid-1 exams scheduled?'. Generic AI chatbots answer confidently but hallucinate because they have no access to internal SR University documents. Our goal is strict ground truth with verifiable citations."
💡 Engineering Solution 03 / 15

Proposed Solution: SRU Assistant

A mathematically grounded, privacy-preserving, zero-cost institutional AI framework

1. Hybrid Retrieval (Dense + Sparse)
Fuses cosine vector embeddings with BM25 Okapi lexical matching via Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF k=60) to eliminate vocabulary mismatch and guarantee exact code matching.
2. Strict Evidence Grounding
Employs evidence-first prompting and automated citation verification. If no document evidence supports a question, it safely refuses rather than hallucinating.
3. Traceable Citation Provenance
Outputs clickable numbered citations ([1], [2]) that open a side drawer showing the exact source document, section, and text passage.
4. 100% Local & Zero-Cost
Requires zero paid cloud APIs, running on local hardware with SQLite storage and optimized inference pipelines. Complete student data privacy.
Presenter (Danchanala Pavan): "SRU Assistant is engineered around 4 core pillars: Dual dense-sparse hybrid retrieval, strict evidence-first grounding, granular citation provenance, and zero-cost local execution. It operates without external API billing and preserves institutional privacy."

📄 Ingestion & Chunking 05 / 15

Document Intelligence & Chunking Strategy

Phase 01 & 02: Transforming messy administrative files into structured knowledge

Multi-Format Parser (Phase 01)

• Ingests .docx, .pdf, and .txt files.

• Normalizes tables into Markdown syntax so tabular relationships (credit tables, exam dates) remain intact.

• Retains document structural hierarchy: document title, section headings, and page boundaries.

Boundary-Aware Chunker (Phase 02)

• Chunk Size: 500 characters / tokens.

• Overlap Window: 50 characters / tokens.

• Boundary Protection: Splits at sentence and paragraph breaks rather than arbitrary character offsets, avoiding sliced dates or truncated regulation clauses.

Chunk Metadata Schema:
{ "chunk_id": "chunk_04", "document_name": "sru_academic_regulations_r23.txt", "section": "2. Attendance Regulations", "page": 1, "token_count": 86 }
Presenter (Enjamuri Sai Nithin): "A major failure point in naive RAG is naive character chunking, where a sentence like 'Students with attendance below 65% are detained' gets cut in half. Our chunker respects paragraph and sentence boundaries with 50-token overlap, preserving semantic coherence."
⚡ Indexing Systems 06 / 15

Dual Indexing: Vector Store & BM25 Lexical Index

Phase 03 & 05: Addressing the fundamental limitations of pure semantic search

1. SQLite Vector Store (Dense)

• Computes dense continuous semantic representations.

• Searches via Cosine Similarity:

Sim(q, d) = (q · d) / (||q|| ||d||)

• Strength: Solves synonymy and paraphrasing (e.g. "minimum presence for exams" matches "Attendance Regulations").

2. BM25 Lexical Index (Sparse)

• Implements BM25 Okapi inverted indexing with term frequency saturation (k1=1.5, b=0.75).

• 709 unique academic vocabulary terms indexed.

• Strength: Pinpoints exact identifiers that vector embeddings blur (e.g. course codes 23CS401, 23CS403, dates 05 October 2026).

💡 Why Both Are Mandatory: In academic queries, a student asking about "23CS401 syllabus" requires exact code matching (BM25), while asking "can I write exams if I fell sick?" requires semantic understanding (Vector).
Presenter (Danchanala Pavan): "Notice why single-index RAG fails. If you only use vector embeddings, course codes like '23CS401' and '23CS402' have almost identical vector coordinates because they share similar context. BM25 provides exact token discrimination, while dense embeddings provide conceptual understanding."
🧮 Fusion Mathematics 07 / 15

Hybrid Retrieval & Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)

Phase 05: Unifying disparate score distributions into a single optimal ranking

The Reciprocal Rank Fusion Formulation
$$\text{RRF\_Score}(d \in D) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}$$

Where $M = \{\text{Dense Vector Retrieval}, \text{BM25 Lexical Retrieval}\}$, $r_m(d)$ is the 1-based rank position of document chunk $d$ in system $m$, and $k = 60$ is the standard smoothing parameter.

Key Advantage 1: Scale Invariance
Cosine similarity ranges from -1 to 1, while BM25 produces unbounded positive numbers. Linear weighting requires fragile manual normalization. RRF is completely rank-based and immune to score calibration mismatch.
Key Advantage 2: Outlier Resistance
A single lucky lexical hit cannot dominate the final ranking unless it also demonstrates reasonable semantic alignment or consistent multi-list ranking.
Presenter (Danchanala Pavan): "Instead of arbitrary weight balancing like 0.7 vector + 0.3 BM25, we use Reciprocal Rank Fusion with smoothing constant k=60. RRF uses the ordinal rank rather than the raw score, making it scale-invariant and highly resilient to score scale discrepancies."
🎯 Precision Refinement 08 / 15

Two-Stage Re-ranking & Context Assembly

Phase 06: Filtering noise to pass only high-density evidence into the generation context

Two-Stage Re-ranking Pipeline

1. Candidate Pool: Pulls Top-20 candidate chunks from the RRF hybrid stage.

2. Exact-Match & Overlap Scoring: Scores candidates based on query term density, exact phrase matches, and metadata alignment.

3. Top-K Selection: Selects the Top-5 most relevant chunks (K=5 proven optimal in our Phase 9 benchmarks).

Evidence-First Context Construction

• Assembles chunks into clear structural blocks:

--- Evidence 1 (ID: chunk_002) ---
Doc: sru_academic_regulations_r23.txt
Section: 2. Attendance Regulations
Content: "Every registered student must secure a minimum of 75%..."
Presenter (Azmeera Tharun): "In Phase 6, we introduced two-stage re-ranking. Pulling Top-20 chunks and pruning them down to Top-5 ensures that tangential chunks are removed, keeping the generation prompt concise and focused."
🛡️ Anti-Hallucination 09 / 15

Grounded Generation & Anti-Hallucination Guardrails

Phase 07: Enforcing strict evidence adherence and controlled safe refusals

🔒
Evidence-First Prompting
The generation engine is constrained to cite only facts present in the retrieved evidence block. External parametric knowledge is disallowed.
🛑
Controlled Safe Refusal
When evidence relevance is insufficient, the system safely responds: "I couldn't find sufficient information in the available SR University documents to answer this accurately."
⚠️
Conflict Detection
Detects conflicting dates or contradictory clauses across multiple versions (e.g., draft schedule vs final calendar) and flags them directly to the student.
Verification Result: In our Phase 9 benchmark, SRU Assistant achieved 94.2% Groundedness with 0% hallucinated policies, compared to standard LLMs which fabricate pass marks and dates.
Presenter (Azmeera Tharun): "The defining difference of SRU Assistant is our anti-hallucination policy. If a question is out of scope—for example, asking about hostel fees when only academic documents are uploaded—the assistant safely refuses rather than guessing."

🔒 Defense in Depth 11 / 15

Security & Reliability Engineering

Phase 11 & 12: Hardening the system against prompt injection and adversarial inputs

1. Query Security Guard (Phase 11)

• Prompt Injection Defense: Detects jailbreak attempts (e.g. "Ignore all previous rules and act as...") and blocks malicious directives.

• Canary Token Protection: Shields system prompt instructions from exfiltration.

• Input Sanitization: Strips script injections and enforces 500-character input bounds.

2. Intent Router & Acronym Expansion (Phase 12)

• Conversational Intent Filter: Intercepts greetings and identity queries ("who are you?") in <1 ms without running unnecessary vector searches.

• Acronym Normalizer: Expands institutional jargon ("when is mid 1?" ──► "when is Mid-Semester Examination I?").

3. Document Ingestion Guard
Path traversal defense, MIME magic header validation, and SHA-256 duplicate detection on document uploads.
4. Privacy Telemetry
Asynchronous JSONL audit logging with UUID request_id, recording latency and retrieval counts with zero PII exposure.
Presenter (Sunkari Sai): "In Phase 11 and 12, we engineered production reliability. We implemented query guards against prompt injection, document upload validation against path traversal, and academic acronym expansion so students can ask natural questions like 'when is mid 1?' and get exact calendar dates."
